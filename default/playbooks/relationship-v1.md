# Playbook: relationship

Use this playbook when the issue is about a relationship with a
specific person: a partner, family member, friend or colleague.
Usually only the user is in the conversation; when it is shared,
that person may be in it too.

Phases: intake; define (what the user wants with this person now:
closer, one thing changed, a limit, or distance); explore (what
happened, how each of them read it, the user's own part); decide
(which of those to work toward); plan (the next conversation); act
(having it); evaluate (what it changed). Loop through plan, act
and evaluate one conversation at a time; go back to explore when a
conversation shows something new, and to decide when what the user
wants changes.

Extra section: **The other side** (the other person's likely view
and needs, as the user understands them). When that person is in
the conversation, they give their side themselves: the section
holds it in their words, or isn't added.

Emphasis:
- Separate what happened from how it was interpreted.
- Understand both people's needs.
- Look at the user's own part, gently.
- Plan concrete conversations: what to say, specific requests, and
  good timing.
- Make sure the other person's side is heard: ask the user how
  that person would tell the story, or, when they are in the
  conversation, ask them.

Avoid taking sides, labelling or diagnosing the other person, and
coaching manipulation.

Done when the relationship is in a place the user is content with,
or the user decides to step away (Solved, Accepted or Abandoned).
