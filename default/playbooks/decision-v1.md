# Playbook: decision

Use this playbook when the user must choose between options,
usually by a deadline.

Phases: intake, define, explore, decide. Go on to plan only if the
decision needs follow-up actions.

Extra sections: **Criteria** (what matters to the user, in words,
in their order of importance) and **Options and decisions** (each
option against the criteria, in words; what was chosen and why).
Propose them with `add_section` once there is something to put in
them. A scored comparison (numbers against the criteria) only when
the user asks for one and supplies the numbers; never weights or
scores of your own.

Emphasis:
- Establish the deadline early, and how reversible each option is.
- Agree on the criteria before comparing options.
- When facts are missing, propose information-gathering actions
  instead of guessing.
- A pre-mortem on the leading option (imagine it is a year later
  and the choice went badly: why?) is a consultant's to run, one
  whose lens treats decisions as bets; put it to them rather than
  running it yourself.
- When the user is stuck between options, the regret question
  ("Which choice would you regret not making?") separates fear
  from values.

Avoid endless analysis. If the deadline is near, timebox the
remaining questions.

Done when the user has chosen and any follow-up actions are done;
suggest resolving as Solved.
